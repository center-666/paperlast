\section{Fixed model, gain assumption and time indices}
\label{rev:model}
Let
\[
 x_{k+1}=Ax_k+w_k,\qquad y_{i,k}=H_ix_k+v_{i,k},
\]
where $x_k\in\R^n$, $y_{i,k}\in\R^m$, and all current and historical
process/measurement noises are independent zero-mean Gaussian variables,
with $Q\succ0$, $R_i\succ0$ and a Gaussian independent initial state.
The trusted local measurement is not attacked. The graph is the original
connected, undirected graph without self-loops. The common dimension $m$
is retained so that the original neighbor sum is defined.
Each $(A,H_i)$ is assumed observable as in the protected paper; the
weaker aggregate or network-only observability assumption is not
silently substituted. Local observability is a declared scope restriction,
not a claim that it is necessary for every possible estimator.

For prescribed gains, the original update is exactly
\begin{align}
 \widehat x_{i,k}^{-}&=A\widehat x_{i,k-1}^{+},\label{rev:prediction}\\
 \widehat x_{i,k}^{+}&=\widehat x_{i,k}^{-}
 +K_{i,k}(y_{i,k}-H_i\widehat x_{i,k}^{-})\notag\\
 &\quad+G_{i,k}\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i}
       (\bar y_{ji,k}-H_j\widehat x_{i,k}^{-}).\label{rev:commonG}
\end{align}
The bar denotes the actual decoded neighbor packet. Equivalently, the
measurement, observation matrix, gain and measurement covariance are
\begin{align}
 \widehat y_{i,k}&=\col\left(y_{i,k},\sum_j\bar y_{ji,k}\right),&
 \widehat H_i&=\col\left(H_i,\sum_j H_j\right),\notag\\
 \widehat K_{i,k}&=[K_{i,k}\ G_{i,k}],&
 \widehat R_i&=\operatorname{diag}\left(R_i,\sum_jR_j\right).
 \label{rev:aggregate}
\end{align}
This is not a filter that stacks every neighbor with an independent gain.
An equivalent full stack is possible only with the constrained repeated
gain $[K_{i,k}\ G_{i,k}\cdots G_{i,k}]$.

The protected source supplies no complete Riccati/gain design.
If the five-node reference uses the Niu aggregate-MMSE design, this is an
explicit supplemental assumption. Its saved gain matrices do not identify
the authors' historical 50-node run. In the fixed-gain results below,
set $K_{i,k}=K_i$, $G_{i,k}=G_i$ and
\begin{equation}
 J_i=I-K_iH_i-G_i\sum_jH_j,\qquad
 F_i=J_iA,\qquad \Phi_i=AJ_i.\label{rev:Fphi}
\end{equation}
Schur stability, when needed, is an additional checked condition, not a
consequence of graph connectivity alone.

\begin{proposition}[Posterior and prediction displacements]
\label{rev:bias}
Use the same physical state and noise realization for the nominal and
attacked copies of \eqref{rev:commonG}. Assume that every link is accepted,
the gains are fixed and the only altered link is $j\to i$.
Define $b_k^+=\widehat x_{i,k}^{a,+}-\widehat x_{i,k}^{0,+}$ and
$b_k^-=\widehat x_{i,k}^{a,-}-\widehat x_{i,k}^{0,-}$.
If $\eta_k=\bar y_{ji,k}^{a}-\bar y_{ji,k}^{0}$, then
\begin{align}
 b_k^-&=A b_{k-1}^+, &
 b_k^+&=J_i b_k^-+G_i\eta_k,\label{rev:biasstep}\\
 b_k^+&=F_i b_{k-1}^++G_i\eta_k,&
 b_{k+1}^-&=\Phi_i b_k^-+AG_i\eta_k.\label{rev:biasrec}
\end{align}
The matrices $F_i$ and $\Phi_i$ have the same characteristic polynomial,
but in general they are different matrices with different induced norms.
They describe different output times.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Subtract the two prediction and update equations. All physical-noise terms
cancel because the copies use the same raw realization. This gives
\eqref{rev:biasstep}, and substitution gives \eqref{rev:biasrec}.
The characteristic-polynomial identity follows from
$\det(I-zJ_iA)=\det(I-zAJ_i)$ and polynomial continuation.
No invertibility of $A$ is required.
\end{proof}

Consequently, a posterior danger threshold cannot be applied unchanged
to $b_k^-$, or to $b_{k+1}^-$, by merely renaming $F_i$ as $\Phi_i$.
For $e_k^0=x_k-\widehat x_{i,k}^{0,+}$, the exact MSE difference is
\begin{equation}
 \E\|e_k^a\|^2-\E\|e_k^0\|^2
 =\E\|b_k^+\|^2-2\E[(e_k^0)^Tb_k^+].\label{rev:MSE}
\end{equation}
It equals $\|b_k^+\|^2$ for a deterministic displacement and an unbiased
nominal filter. It equals $\E\|b_k^+\|^2$ for secret-dependent
displacements only when the stated independence/unbiasedness eliminates
the cross term. Data-dependent isolation does not satisfy this condition
automatically.

\section{Parity residual, exact nominal covariance and an online score}
\label{rev:scoresection}
Fix the transmitted source $j$ and order $s\ge1$. Let
\[
 \mathcal O_s=\col(H_j,H_jA,\ldots,H_jA^s),\qquad
 \rank\mathcal O_s=n,\qquad d=(s+1)m-n>0.
\]
The rows of $Z=[Z_0\ \cdots\ Z_s]\in\R^{d\times(s+1)m}$
form a full basis of the left null space of $\mathcal O_s$.
The healthy window and residual are
\begin{align}
 Y_k^0&=\mathcal O_s x_{k-s}+\mathcal W_s W_k+V_k,&
 r_k^0&=ZY_k^0,\label{rev:window}\\
 W_k&=\col(w_{k-s},\ldots,w_{k-1}),&
 V_k&=\col(v_{j,k-s},\ldots,v_{j,k}).\notag
\end{align}
Thus there are exactly $s$ relevant process-noise vectors and $s+1$
measurement-noise vectors. With row $a=0,\ldots,s$ and column
$b=0,\ldots,s-1$,
\[
 [\mathcal W_s]_{ab}=
 \begin{cases}H_jA^{a-1-b},&b<a,\\0,&b\ge a.\end{cases}
\]
The nominal covariance is
\begin{equation}
 \Theta_s=Z[\mathcal W_s(I_s\otimes Q)\mathcal W_s^T
           +I_{s+1}\otimes R_j]Z^T\succ0.\label{rev:Theta}
\end{equation}
The original extra last process-noise block has a zero coefficient,
so retaining that block with its matching zero column is equivalent;
it must not be mistaken for an additional effective noise sample.

\begin{proposition}[Predetermined single-decision calibration]
\label{rev:calibration}
For a complete, predetermined window under the healthy Gaussian model,
ideal synchronized decoding and exactly known \eqref{rev:Theta},
\begin{equation}
 T_{ji,k}=r_{ji,k}^T\Theta_s^{-1}r_{ji,k}\sim\chi_d^2.
 \label{rev:score}
\end{equation}
The operational alarm $T_{ji,k}>\tau_{\alpha_k}$, where
$\tau_{\alpha_k}=F_{\chi_d^2}^{-1}(1-\alpha_k)$, has nominal size
$\alpha_k$. For a predetermined finite family of decisions $\mathcal I$,
$\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal I}\alpha_\ell\le\alpha_{\rm event}$
implies a whole-family false-alarm bound $\alpha_{\rm event}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The state term is annihilated, and independent Gaussian noises give
$r_k^0\sim\mathcal N(0,\Theta_s)$. Positive definiteness follows because
$R_j\succ0$ and $Z$ has full row rank. Whitening gives $d$ independent
standard Gaussian coordinates. The finite-family statement is the union
bound and does not require independence of overlapping windows.
\end{proof}

The nominal covariance does not contain estimator gains. Overlapping
windows and links nevertheless share noises and need not be independent.
For a stacked record $R=\mathcal L\nu$ built from one primitive-noise
vector with covariance $\Omega$, its covariance is exactly
$\mathcal L\Omega\mathcal L^T$. Individual scores cannot be summed
and assigned independent chi-square degrees of freedom without checking
this joint covariance. Adaptive window selection, estimated covariance,
model mismatch, missing packets and infinite monitoring need separate
calibration. A finite-horizon $5\%$ budget is not an infinite-run guarantee.
The detector uses decoded packets and the nominal covariance; it does not
observe the attacked probability law or the attack labels.

An alarm may drive the original binary gate, but this does not prove a
stable or accurate gated filter. With the same fixed gate path in both
copies, replace $J_i$ by
$J_{i,\varphi}=I-K_iH_i-G_i\sum_j\varphi_{ji,k}H_j$.
If the two gate paths differ, subtracting the actual updates also produces
$G_i\sum_j(\varphi_{ji,k}^a-\varphi_{ji,k}^0)
(y_{j,k}^0-H_j\widehat x_{i,k}^{0,-})$.
Hence fixed-$F_i$ conclusions cannot certify the data-dependent closed loop.

\section{KL definition and conditional versus marginal laws}
\label{rev:KLsection}
For probability measures $P$ and $P_0$,
\begin{equation}
 D(P\|P_0)=\int\log\!\left(\frac{dP}{dP_0}\right)dP
 \quad(P\ll P_0),\qquad D(P\|P_0)=+\infty\quad\hbox{otherwise}.
 \label{rev:KLdefinition}
\end{equation}
For $P_a=\mathcal N(\mu_a,\Sigma_a)$ and
$P_b=\mathcal N(\mu_b,\Sigma_b)$ with positive definite covariances,
the usual trace/log-determinant formula applies. That formula is a
special case of \eqref{rev:KLdefinition}, not a definition for arbitrary
random variables.

For a fixed deterministic unencoded window injection $\Delta$,
$r^a=r^0+Z\Delta$ gives exactly
\begin{equation}
 D(\mathcal L(r^a)\|\mathcal L(r^0))
 =\tfrac12\Delta^TZ^T\Theta_s^{-1}Z\Delta.\label{rev:shiftKL}
\end{equation}
For synchronized multiplicative encoding,
\begin{equation}
 u_t=M_t^{-1}y_{j,t},\quad u_t^a=u_t+a_t,\quad
 \bar y_{ji,t}^a=M_tu_t^a=y_{j,t}+M_ta_t.\label{rev:encoding}
\end{equation}
The code-domain injection is $a_t$; its decoded effect is $\eta_t=M_ta_t$.
Write $\mathcal M=\operatorname{diag}(M_0,\ldots,M_s)$ and
$U=Z\mathcal M\Delta$. For deterministic $\Delta$ independent of
the secret and physical noises, $r^a=r^0+U$ is Gaussian conditioned
on the entire secret realization, but its marginal law is generally
a non-Gaussian mixture.

Let $S$ denote the secret window and let the nominal joint law be
$P_0\otimes P_S$. Then
\begin{equation}
 D(P_{r^a,S}\|P_0\otimes P_S)
 =\E_S D(P_{r^a\mid S}\|P_0)
 =D(P_{r^a}\|P_0)+I(r^a;S).\label{rev:jointKL}
\end{equation}
For fixed deterministic $\Delta$, the middle term is
$\frac12\E[U^T\Theta_s^{-1}U]$. Thus conditional/joint and marginal
KL are not interchangeable. For random attacks, even the conditional law
need not be Gaussian. None of these distribution-level expressions is
an observed per-packet KL statistic.

\section{Correct strict-positivity and non-Gaussian bounds}
\label{rev:positivity}
Use the already adopted mechanism
\[
 M_t=\lambda I_m+\beta_t\beta_t^T,\qquad
 \beta_t\overset{\rm iid}{\sim}\mathcal N(0,qI_m),\quad
 \lambda>0,\quad q=\sigma_\beta^2>0.
\]
The whole watermark sequence is independent of the physical initial state
and every physical process and measurement noise.
The whole attack window $\Delta$ has finite second moment and is independent
of the \emph{joint} element $(r^0,\beta_0,\ldots,\beta_s)$.
Knowing neither the current secret nor its seed does not imply this
assumption for a packet-dependent attacker. For fixed deterministic $a$,
Gaussian fourth moments give
\[
 \E[\beta\beta^Ta]=qa,\qquad
 \Cov(\beta\beta^Ta)=q^2(aa^T+\|a\|^2I_m).
\]
Consequently, with
$D(\Delta)=\operatorname{diag}(\delta_\ell\delta_\ell^T+
\|\delta_\ell\|^2I_m)_{\ell=0}^s$,
\begin{align}
 \mu_U&=(\lambda+q)Z\E\Delta,\label{rev:mu}\\
 C_U&=q^2Z\E[D(\Delta)]Z^T
       +(\lambda+q)^2Z\Cov(\Delta)Z^T.\label{rev:C}
\end{align}
Conditioning on the whole $\Delta$, using independent watermark blocks,
and applying total covariance proves these formulas, including temporal
attack correlation. Independence from the nominal residual gives
$\E r^a=\mu_U$ and $\Cov(r^a)=\Theta_s+C_U$.

\begin{lemma}[The required time-block condition]
\label{rev:c1}
For deterministic $\Delta=\col(\delta_0,\ldots,\delta_s)$, define
\begin{align}
 c_1(\Delta)&=\tr(\Theta_s^{-1}ZD(\Delta)Z^T)\notag\\
 &=\sum_{\ell=0}^s\left[
   \|\Theta_s^{-1/2}Z_\ell\delta_\ell\|^2
   +\|\delta_\ell\|^2\|\Theta_s^{-1/2}Z_\ell\|_F^2\right].
 \label{rev:c1formula}
\end{align}
Then $c_1(\Delta)>0$ if and only if there is an index $\ell$ with
$\delta_\ell\ne0$ and $Z_\ell\ne0$. Full row rank of $Z$ and
nonzero positive semidefinite $D$ alone do not prove strict positivity.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expand the block trace. Each displayed summand is nonnegative; its second
term is strictly positive exactly when both indicated factors are nonzero.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[A counterexample inside the original model class]
\label{rev:degenerate}
Let $n=m=1$, $A=0$, $H_j=1$, $s=1$ and $Q,R_j>0$.
Then $(A,H_j)$ is locally observable,
$\mathcal O_1=(1,0)^T$ has full column rank, and $Z=[0\ 1]$ has full
row rank. For $\xi\ne0$ take $\Delta=\mathcal O_1\xi=(\xi,0)^T$.
Although $D=\operatorname{diag}(2\xi^2,0)\ne0$, $ZDZ^T=0$ and
$c_1=0$. The first time block $Z_0$ is zero. For every secret realization
$Z\mathcal M\Delta=0$, so this complete window remains unchanged.
This does not assert a blind direction in the three previously tested
nondegenerate models, or stealthiness of every transition window.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[Correct marginal KL lower bound]
\label{rev:entropy}
Under the stated independence and moment assumptions let
$P_*=\mathcal L(r^a)$, $P_0=\mathcal N(0,\Theta_s)$ and
$B=\Theta_s^{-1/2}C_U\Theta_s^{-1/2}\succeq0$. Then
\begin{align}
 D(P_*\|P_0)&\ge\tfrac12\left[
 \tr(B)-\log\det(I+B)+\mu_U^T\Theta_s^{-1}\mu_U\right],
 \label{rev:entropybound}\\
 D(P_*\|P_0)&\ge\frac d2 f\!\left(\frac{\tr B}{d}\right),
 \qquad f(x)=x-\log(1+x).\label{rev:traceKL}
\end{align}
For a fixed deterministic $\Delta$ not depending on $q$, with
$Z\Delta=0$ and $c_1(\Delta)>0$, the right side is
$\frac d2 f(q^2c_1/d)$ and tends to infinity as $q\to\infty$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Gaussian convolution gives a density. The covariance is finite; its entropy
is bounded above by the entropy of the matched Gaussian and bounded below
by that of the independent Gaussian summand. Equivalently, subtracting
the matched-Gaussian cross entropy yields
$D(P_*\|P_0)\ge D(\mathcal N(\mu_U,\Theta_s+C_U)\|P_0)$.
This is \eqref{rev:entropybound}, not a Gaussian equality for $P_*$.
Apply convexity of $f$ to the $d$ nonnegative eigenvalues of $B$ and drop
the nonnegative mean term. For a fixed stealthy deterministic attack,
$\mu_U=0$ and $\tr B=q^2c_1$. The function $f$ is increasing and unbounded.
\end{proof}

KL growth is not an observed alarm. If needed, the stage proof of
fixed-attack asymptotic score power can be reused: couple
$\beta_\ell=\sqrt q\,z_\ell$ and write $r^a=r^0+qV(z)$.
For $c_1>0$ at least one component of $V$ is a nonzero polynomial,
so $\Pr(V=0)=0$. Then the score divided by $q^2$ converges almost surely
to $V^T\Theta_s^{-1}V>0$, and bounded convergence gives power tending
to one for a fixed threshold. This is pointwise in a fixed attack and
specified window, not a finite-$q$ guarantee, a uniform P3 guarantee,
or a bound on danger before the first alarm.

\section{Finite support: interior and transition windows}
\label{rev:support}
Retain the original autonomous attack, with a finite onset and duration:
\begin{equation}
 a_{T_a+t}=H_jA^t\xi\quad(0\le t<L),\qquad
 a_k=0\quad\hbox{otherwise}.\label{rev:autonomous}
\end{equation}
No future measurement or secret realization is used.
The true code-domain energy is
\begin{equation}
 \sum_k\|a_k\|^2=\xi^TW_L\xi,\qquad
 W_L=\sum_{t=0}^{L-1}(H_jA^t)^T(H_jA^t).\label{rev:energy}
\end{equation}
The formula changes with $L$ because the same physical sum contains
a different number of injections; it does not change the energy definition.

Define the complete finite-support injection matrix
$T_L=\col(H_j,H_jA,\ldots,H_jA^{L-1})$. For any declared monitor end
time $k$, let $E_k$ select from a length-$L$ supported sequence the
positions $k-s,\ldots,k$, filling positions outside
$[T_a,T_a+L-1]$ with zeros. Thus
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_k=E_kT_L\xi,\qquad
 R_k=ZE_kT_L,\qquad
 \mathscr R_{\mathcal H,L}=\col(R_k)_{k\in\mathcal H}.
 \label{rev:finiteR}
\end{equation}
When healthy prehistory is retained and all affected windows are evaluated,
the end-time set is $\mathcal H=\{T_a,\ldots,T_a+L-1+s\}$.
Only available complete windows may be included if monitoring begins late;
that different set must be declared.

\begin{proposition}[Exact finite-support and useful-direction conditions]
\label{rev:finitesupport}
For deterministic unencoded \eqref{rev:autonomous}, exact residual
preservation at every specified $k\in\mathcal H$ holds if and only if
$\xi\in\ker\mathscr R_{\mathcal H,L}$.
A nonzero effective injection with this property exists if and only if
\begin{equation}
 \ker\mathscr R_{\mathcal H,L}\not\subseteq\ker T_L.\label{rev:effective}
\end{equation}
Let $\mathscr B$ stack the original receiver's displacement maps at the
declared damage times, with zero initial displacement and fixed gains.
For a posterior evaluation at relative time $h\ge0$, its corresponding
block is explicitly
\[
 B_h^+=\sum_{t=0}^{\min(h,L-1)}F_i^{h-t}G_iH_jA^t,
 \qquad b_{T_a+h}^+=B_h^+\xi.
\]
For a prediction evaluation, use $B_h^-=AB_{h-1}^+$ for $h\ge1$
and $B_0^-=0$; do not assign a posterior threshold to the prediction map.
A harmful exactly residual-preserving attack exists if and only if
\begin{equation}
 \ker\mathscr R_{\mathcal H,L}\not\subseteq\ker\mathscr B.
 \label{rev:harm}
\end{equation}
For any positive finite energy budget, a nonzero witness of the latter
condition can be scaled to satisfy that budget. This last statement
does not guarantee reaching a positive prescribed danger threshold.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The deterministic shift at $k$ is $R_k\xi$ and the nominal covariance
is unchanged. Equality of Gaussian means is equivalent to equality of
the specified marginal laws, and stacking gives the first assertion.
The next two assertions state precisely the existence of a vector in
one kernel but not in the relevant ineffective kernel. In particular,
$\ker T_L\subseteq\ker\mathscr B$ for zero initial displacement.
Scaling a fixed nonzero witness scales its injection energy quadratically.
\end{proof}

For an interior window $T_a+s\le k<T_a+L$,
$\Delta_k=\mathcal O_sA^{k-s-T_a}\xi$, so $R_k\xi=0$.
This identity does not extend across onset or termination. For example,
$A=H_j=1$, $s=1$, $Z=[-1\ 1]$, $T_a=0$, $L=2$ and $\xi=1$
give shifts $1,0,-1$ at end times $0,1,2$, respectively.
Both boundary samples matter. With watermarking, replace $R_k\xi$ by
$Z\mathcal M_kE_kT_L\xi$; unencoded invisibility cannot be claimed for
this random decoded perturbation.

These are standard finite-dimensional kernel/linear-output conditions.
They sharpen the assumptions and quantifiers for R3.3, but do not close
R1.1. Finite-support Toeplitz and boundary analyses already appear in
Martynova--Zhang (2019), Li et al. (2024), and Zhang et al. (2023).

\section{Unit-circle modes and natural decay after termination}
\label{rev:unitcircle}
Use the prediction displacement $p_t=b_{T_a+t}^-$, $p_0=0$ and
unencoded persistent autonomous input. Equations \eqref{rev:biasrec} give
\begin{equation}
 p_t=\sum_{u=0}^{t-1}\Phi_i^{t-1-u}AG_iH_jA^u\xi.
 \label{rev:convolution}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[The missing spectral boundary]
\label{rev:bounded}
Assume $\Phi_i$ is Schur. If $A$ is Schur, $p_t\to0$.
If $A$ is power bounded, then $p_t$ is bounded. In particular, for $A=I$,
\begin{equation}
 p_t\longrightarrow(I-\Phi_i)^{-1}AG_iH_j\xi,\label{rev:Aidentity}
\end{equation}
which need not be zero. Spectrum in the closed unit disk with semisimple
unit-circle eigenvalues is sufficient for power boundedness. Merely
assuming $\rho(A)\le1$ is insufficient.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose $C_\Phi<\infty$ and $r_\Phi<1$ with
$\|\Phi_i^t\|\le C_\Phi r_\Phi^t$. If
$\|A^t\|\le C_A r_A^t$ for $r_A<1$, the convolution is bounded
by a constant times $t\max(r_\Phi,r_A)^{t-1}\to0$.
If $\sup_t\|A^t\|\le B_A$, use the convergent geometric sum for
$\Phi_i$ instead. For $A=I$, the geometric matrix sum yields
\eqref{rev:Aidentity}. The spectral statement is the finite-dimensional
Jordan characterization of power-bounded matrices.
\end{proof}

The stage example is retained without changing the physical model class:
$H_i=H_j=I_2$, $K_i=0$, $G_i=I_2$ and
\[
 A=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Then $J_i=0$, $F_i=\Phi_i=0$. For $\xi=(0,1)^T$,
$b_{T_a+t}^+=(t,1)^T$ for $t\ge0$ and
$p_t=(t,1)^T$ for $t\ge1$. A unit-circle Jordan chain gives polynomial
growth despite having no eigenvalue outside the unit disk.
Choosing $\xi=(1,0)^T$ gives bounded displacement in the same model;
Jordan-chain presence alone does not imply growth for every attack.
These statements concern the persistent input, not a finite attack.

The existing real-unstable-mode statement also needs its full separation
condition: if $Av=\lambda_u v$, $\lambda_u\in\R$, $|\lambda_u|>1$,
$\xi=cv+\widetilde\xi$ with $c\ne0$ and
$\|A^t\widetilde\xi\|\le C\rho^t$ for $\rho<|\lambda_u|$, and
$AG_iH_jv\ne0$, then the persistent-input prediction bias satisfies
\[
 \frac{p_t}{\lambda_u^{t-1}}
 \longrightarrow c(I-\lambda_u^{-1}\Phi_i)^{-1}AG_iH_jv\ne0.
\]
The mode contribution is a convergent geometric sum after normalization;
the remainder is $o(|\lambda_u|^{t-1})$ by the strict separation bound.
Tied leading modes, complex pairs and generalized eigenvectors do not
satisfy this statement merely because $\rho(A)>1$.

If the attack terminates with last injected packet at $T_b-1=T_a+L-1$,
then for $r\ge0$
\begin{equation}
 b_{T_b-1+r}^+=F_i^r b_{T_b-1}^+.\label{rev:stop}
\end{equation}
Thus after any finite autonomous attack a Schur $F_i$ makes the residual
displacement decay, even when $A$ is unstable or has a Jordan block.
Non-normal $F_i$ can still have transient amplification; Schur stability
alone is not a monotone Euclidean-norm bound. A quantitative bound requires
a suitable norm certificate or $\|F_i^r\|\le Cr_0^r$ with a stated $C$.
Stopping or natural decay does not undo a dangerous output already sent.

\section{Law budget, energy and notation repairs}
\label{rev:budget}
The parameter $\theta$ is a distributional attack constraint,
$D(P_*\|P_0)\le\theta$; it is not the operational score threshold.
Assume the whole-window independence in Section~\ref{rev:positivity} and
\begin{equation}
 c_Z=\min_{0\le\ell\le s}
        \tr(\Theta_s^{-1}Z_\ell Z_\ell^T)>0.\label{rev:cZ}
\end{equation}
This means that every time block is nonzero; full column rank of each
block is not required.

\begin{proposition}[The meaning of the existing energy theorem]
\label{rev:energybound}
Under these conditions, each $q>0$ and finite $\theta\ge0$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
 D(P_*\|P_0)\le\theta
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \E\|\Delta\|^2\le
 \frac{d f^{-1}(2\theta/d)}{c_Zq^2}.
 \label{rev:windowenergy}
\end{equation}
The bound controls the code-domain window injection. It is not a direct
bound on decoded energy, posterior MSE, delay or danger risk.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
From \eqref{rev:C} and the definition of $D(\Delta)$,
\[
 \tr B\ge q^2\sum_\ell\E\|\delta_\ell\|^2
                \tr(\Theta_s^{-1}Z_\ell Z_\ell^T)
 \ge q^2c_Z\E\|\Delta\|^2.
\]
Equation \eqref{rev:traceKL} and the budget imply
$f(\tr B/d)\le2\theta/d$. The continuous strictly increasing function
$f$ has an inverse on $[0,\infty)$, proving \eqref{rev:windowenergy}.
\end{proof}

Families $\Delta(q)$ may respond to the public design, provided the
whole-window independence holds separately for every $q$. This is not a
theorem for payload-feedback attacks. Nor does fixed-$\Delta$ asymptotic
power prove uniform P3 power. For fixed deterministic $a$,
\begin{equation}
 \E\|M_ta\|^2=
 [\lambda^2+2\lambda q+(m+2)q^2]\|a\|^2.\label{rev:decodedenergy}
\end{equation}
Taking $a=b/q$ shows that vanishing code-domain energy alone does not
force the decoded effect to vanish. It does not by itself establish
feasibility of a prescribed KL budget. The two concepts must remain separate.
